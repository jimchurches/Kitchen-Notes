# Brown Butter Chocolate Chip Cookies

## Overview

A rich, chewy chocolate chip cookie made with browned butter, brown sugar, vanilla, and good dark or semi-sweet chocolate.

The browned butter gives the cookies a nutty, deeper flavour. Chilling the dough helps the texture and flavour, and the cookies are best baked slightly underdone so they stay soft in the centre with lightly crisp edges.

Makes about 15 cookies.

## Ingredients (metric/Australian)

- 170 g unsalted butter
- 210 g light brown sugar, packed
- 50 g white sugar
- 1 large egg, at room temperature
- 1 large egg yolk, at room temperature
- 1 tablespoon pure vanilla extract
- 220 g plain flour
- ¾ teaspoon bicarbonate of soda
- ¾ teaspoon kosher salt, or fine salt if that is what you have
- 210 g semi-sweet or dark chocolate chips, chunks, or roughly chopped chocolate
- Flaky sea salt, for sprinkling

### Notes on ingredients

- Use unsalted butter so you control the salt level.
- Good chocolate matters. A chopped chocolate block, good chocolate chunks, or quality dark/semi-sweet chips will work better than cheap compound chocolate.
- Brown sugar helps keep the cookies soft and chewy.
- White sugar helps the edges crisp slightly.
- Vanilla and salt are important. Do not skip them.

## Oven temps and cooking temps when relevant

- Oven: **180°C conventional** or **160°C fan-forced**
- Bake time: **12–13 minutes**
- Chill time: at least **1 hour**, or overnight if convenient

The cookies should be light golden around the edges but still soft in the middle when removed from the oven. They continue to set as they cool.

## Instructions

1. Place the butter in a small saucepan over medium-low heat.

2. Cook gently, stirring and scraping the bottom and sides every 10–15 seconds.

3. The butter will melt, foam, and then start to smell nutty. Continue cooking until it turns golden brown and small brown flecks appear.

4. Remove from the heat and let the browned butter cool for about 5 minutes. It should be warm, not scalding.

5. For precision, the browned butter should weigh about **140 g** after cooking. If it is much lower, add a little milk to bring it back to 140 g.

6. In a large mixing bowl, combine the browned butter, brown sugar, and white sugar.

7. Add the egg, egg yolk, and vanilla. Mix well.

8. Add the flour, bicarbonate of soda, and salt.

9. Fold gently until no streaks of flour remain. Do not overmix.

10. Fold through the chocolate.

11. Cover the bowl and chill the dough for at least 1 hour, or overnight.

12. Preheat the oven to **180°C conventional** or **160°C fan-forced**.

13. Line two baking trays with baking paper.

14. Remove the dough from the fridge and let it soften at room temperature for about 20 minutes, or until scoopable.

15. Scoop into balls of roughly equal size. A 1½-ounce scoop is ideal, but a heaped tablespoon also works.

16. Place dough balls on the trays with room to spread.

17. Bake for **12–13 minutes**, or until the cookies are puffed and lightly golden around the edges but still soft in the middle.

18. Remove from the oven and sprinkle with flaky sea salt if using.

19. Let the cookies cool on the tray for a few minutes before moving or serving.

## Serving ideas and enhancements

- Serve warm while the chocolate is still soft.
- A glass of cold milk is the obvious pairing.
- For a deeper flavour, chill the dough overnight.
- For bigger chocolate pools, use chopped dark chocolate instead of chips.
- For a sweeter version, use semi-sweet chocolate.
- For a more grown-up version, use dark chocolate and a little extra flaky salt.
- The dough balls can be frozen and baked later. Let frozen dough sit at room temperature for about 30 minutes before baking.
- Baked cookies can also be frozen, but the dough freezes better.
- Store baked cookies in an airtight container for up to 3 days, though they are best in the first day or two.

### Troubleshooting

- If the cookies are thick and cakey, there may be too much flour or not enough liquid after browning the butter.
- If the cookies spread too much or seem greasy, the butter may have been too hot when mixed, or the dough may not have chilled long enough.
- If the cookies are dry, they may have been overbaked or overmixed.
- When in doubt, slightly underbake rather than overbake.
