# Sour Cream Shortcrust Pastry

## Overview

A simple sour cream shortcrust pastry made with flour, butter, and sour cream.

This pastry is rich, buttery, flaky, and useful for tarts and pies. It works well as a base recipe for both sweet and savoury fillings. The sour cream helps bring the dough together and gives the pastry a tender texture.

Makes enough for one tart or pie shell.

## Ingredients (metric/Australian)

- 250 g plain flour
- 200 g unsalted butter, chilled and diced
- 125 ml sour cream

## Oven temps and cooking temps when relevant

- Blind baking: 220°C fan-forced
- Conventional oven: 240°C conventional
- Initial blind bake: 15 minutes
- Final blind bake after removing weights: 10 minutes
- Pastry thickness: about 5 mm
- Refrigeration time: at least 20 minutes
- Optional freezer time before baking: 20 minutes

## Instructions

1. Dice the chilled butter.

2. Place the flour and butter in a food processor.

3. Pulse until the mixture resembles fine breadcrumbs.

4. Add the sour cream.

5. Continue pulsing until the dough starts to come together into a ball.

6. Wrap the dough in plastic wrap.

7. Refrigerate for at least 20 minutes.

8. Roll the chilled pastry out to about 5 mm thick.

9. Carefully line the tart or pie tin.

10. Press the pastry gently into the edges.

11. Trim the pastry, leaving about 2 cm above the top of the tin to allow for shrinkage.

12. Prick the base with a fork to reduce puffing.

13. For best results, freeze the lined pastry shell for 20 minutes before baking.

14. Line the pastry with baking paper.

15. Fill with baking beads, rice, chickpeas, or lentils.

16. Bake at 220°C fan-forced for 15 minutes.

17. Remove the baking paper and weights.

18. Bake for another 10 minutes, or until the pastry is cooked and lightly golden.

19. Remove and cool before adding the filling.

## Serving ideas and enhancements

- Use this pastry as the base for pecan pie, fruit tarts, quiches, savoury pies, and other filled tart shells.
- Keep the butter cold before mixing for the best flaky texture.
- Do not overwork the dough.
- Flour can vary in dryness, so the dough may need small adjustments. If it seems too dry, add a little more sour cream. If too soft, add a little flour while rolling.
- Freezing the lined pastry shell before baking helps reduce shrinkage.
- Try to add filling when the filling and pastry are at a similar temperature.
- For sweet pies, the pastry does not need added sugar if the filling is already rich or sweet.
